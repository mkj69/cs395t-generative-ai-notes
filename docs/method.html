Editorial method

How a thought
becomes a note.

This guide governs research notes. Course learning notes use a separate manual workflow and become research notes only when reconstruction produces a question worth pursuing.

CLAIM LABELS

DIRECT
Reported or demonstrated by a cited source.
DERIVATION
Worked out explicitly from stated assumptions.
SYNTHESIS
An interpretation supported across sources.
HYPOTHESIS
A testable conjecture, not an established result.

01 / ENTRY CONDITION

Do not begin with “Today we learned…”

Begin with a real intellectual obstruction: two ideas that appear inconsistent, an equation whose behavior is not intuitive, a result that depends on a hidden assumption, or a paper connection that changes the original framing.

A note earns its place here when reconstructing the idea produces something the source did not hand over directly.

02 / RECONSTRUCTION

Close the source before explaining it.

Write the mechanism in your own sequence. Mark the point where memory fails or the argument becomes vague. That gap is more valuable than fluent paraphrase: it tells you what must be derived, tested, or reread.

03 / CLAIM HYGIENE

Keep evidence and imagination adjacent, but distinct.

A strong note can contain speculation. The requirement is that a reader can see where a cited result ends and the author's synthesis begins. Use claim labels when the boundary could otherwise be mistaken.

04 / ENDING

Leave with a sharper unknown.

Record the smallest experiment, counterexample, proof obligation, or reading path that could change your mind. “More work is needed” is not an open question; a discriminating test is.

05 / PUBLISHING CHECK

Before a note becomes public

  • The central question can be stated in one sentence.
  • The note contains original reconstruction, not lecture transcription.
  • Every source-dependent claim has a citation.
  • Inference and hypothesis are labeled honestly.
  • At least one uncertainty or failure mode remains visible.
  • The ending proposes a concrete next intellectual move.
